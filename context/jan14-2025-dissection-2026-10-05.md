# 14 Jan 2025 nationwide outage: Atlas TLS/HTTP built-ins and OONI

5 Oct 2026. Follow-up to `atlas-14jan2025-positive-control-2026-09-23.md`. Pulled through the browser pane (the cloud sandbox and the Mac's sandbox shell both get 403 from the egress proxy for atlas.ripe.net, api.ooni.io and api.cloudflare.com). The reproduction script is `~/shutdown-measurement/whitelist-detector-tests/jan14_dissection.py`, which runs the same pulls from a terminal.

## 1. Timing correction

The ntc.party catalogue places the onset at "15:0x MSK" (12:0x UTC), and both earlier Atlas tests used 11:55–12:40 UTC. The press puts the onset at about 17:00 MSK: ppc.world says it began around 17:00 MSK, and Vedomosti gives restoration at 17:30–18:00 with RKN's confirmation at 18:08 MSK. The Atlas and OONI data below agree with the press: **onset 14:03 UTC (17:03 MSK), recovery 14:29–14:31 UTC**. The earlier msm 7000 and upload-lag nulls were therefore tested on the wrong window. Both are re-checked in §4.

## 2. RIPE Atlas built-ins

Probe sets: the current registry's country_code RU (2,271 IDs, about 500 active in January 2025) and DE (5,240 IDs). Probes that changed country since then are missed.

**12027 is a plain HTTP GET to http://193.0.6.141 (ip-echo.ripe.net), every hour per probe.**

| Window (14 Jan, UTC) | RU results | RU failed | Main error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13:40–14:00, by 5-min bin | 46 / 39 / 50 / 48 | 0 / 0 / 2 / 2 | connect timeouts (background) |
| 14:00 bin | 47 | 12 | first failure at 14:03:09 |
| 14:05 / 14:10 / 14:15 / 14:20 bins | 40 / 45 / 37 / 33 | 36 / 38 / 33 / 30 | `timeout reading status` |
| 14:25 bin | 38 | 31 | same |
| 14:30 bin | 36 | 5 | last failures 14:30:32 |
| 14:35–14:45 | 126 | 2 | background |

14:05–14:25 UTC: 154 RU probes reported. **136 failed, on 98 ASNs**, among them 12389 (Rostelecom), 25513 (MGTS), 8359 (MTS), 3216 (Beeline), 8402 and 31200. 147 of the 152 in-window failures are `timeout reading status`, meaning the TCP connection opened and the request was sent but no response arrived. The same clock window on 13 and 15 Jan: 5 failures in 343 results. **DE control:** 14 failures in 703 results inside the window, 47 in 1,960 outside it (2% in both).

The 18 RU probes that succeeded in 14:05–14:25 are on 17 ASNs (38984, 42289, 59734, 8985, 51724, 49828 ×2, 48149, 20764, 59504, 35807, 39503, 31275, 35237, 210756, 57073, 209847, 48573). Their ISPs have not yet been identified. Any of them could be a network the event did not reach.

**14001/14002 are TLS handshakes (sslcert) to www.ripe.net and atlas.ripe.net, every 12 h per probe.**

From 14:04:12 to 14:26:46 UTC, **37 of the 40 RU results failed**: 35 with `timeout reading hello` (the ClientHello went out and no ServerHello came back) and 1 with a connect timeout. The 18 failing probes are on 16 ASNs. The successes were probe 1009416 (AS205090) twice and probe 55654 at 14:25:39, which then failed at 14:26:46. The last success before onset was at 14:03:10, and from 14:30:59 onward everything succeeded again. `timeout reading hello` appears 42 times in RU over 13–15 Jan, and 35 of those fall in this 23-minute window. The `alert 40` failures on 14002 (atlas.ripe.net) recur at the same rate on every day and are unrelated. **DE control:** 0 of 71 failed on 14001 in the window, and 3 of 72 on 14002 (alert 40, the background rate).

Two signals show up: measurement volume falls to roughly a quarter of the flanking days, and the anomaly share doubles. Why volume falls is not established. Probes may have failed to fetch test lists, run fewer tests, or failed to submit their results.

The plain-HTTP anomaly share does not rise. That does **not** mean plain HTTP worked: a sample of 14 of the in-window plain-HTTP measurements shows 12 with TCP `ok` and `http_experiment_failure = timed_out`, labelled `anomaly=false, blocking=null`. OONI's analysis leaves these unlabelled rather than calling them anomalies. Its anomaly flag therefore under-counts this event class, which is a Stage 1 result about the instrument. Twelve raw HTTPS measurements sampled from the window all show TCP `ok`, then a TLS handshake `generic_timeout_error`, on AS12389, 25513, 8331, 49346 and 50716.

The per-ASN split in the window is small-n. AS12389 had 62/104 anomalous (baseline 119/567), AS35728 45/50, and AS49346 55/61. AS3216 had 0/8 and AS51659 3/29, so either may have been unaffected, but the counts are too small to say.

## 4. Re-check of the earlier Atlas tests on the corrected window

**msm 7000 connection log**: RU disconnects starting 14:00–14:40 UTC on each January day ran 1–25, median 6. On 14 Jan there were 10, and 4 of those came from the flapping probe 1006560. That is null again. Established SSH sessions to the controllers survived, which fits a mechanism where only new connections fail. The missing-disconnect problem since August 2024 (`data-inventory-2026-10-01.md` §4) means these counts are lower bounds.

**Upload lag (msm 1009)**: the RU late-share for the 14:05–14:25 bins was 0.074, 0.047, 0.062, 0.056 and 0.080. Earlier the RU 95th percentile was given as 0.064. Two bins exceed it, the other bins in the window do not, and DE stayed at 0.034–0.050. Ping results kept arriving at the usual rate (n≈376 per bin). The most this supports: weakly elevated, not a detector.

## 5. What the event looked like from outside

In this window, on the networks of most Russian Atlas probes, new TCP connections to foreign hosts completed the handshake, and the first application payload got no response. That held for a TLS ClientHello and for a plain HTTP request to a bare IP. Connections that were already established (the Atlas SSH sessions) carried on, and ICMP (a-root ping) carried on. About 12% of probes were unaffected. Onset was sharp (14:03:09–14:04:55 UTC); recovery took about two minutes (14:28:44–14:30:53 UTC).

The Atlas data does not show whether domestic destinations were affected, because every built-in target is a RIPE host. The OONI .ru HTTPS anomaly share rose from 0.20 to 0.53, which suggests some domestic HTTPS was affected too, but the n is 30, and the ntc.party reports say Russian sites recovered first.

The 4 May 2026 МГТС report (ntc 24403 #23) describes the same pattern: existing connections survived and new ones were refused. Both look like a TSPU state where new flows are dropped after the handshake. That is a hypothesis for the mechanism table, not a finding.
