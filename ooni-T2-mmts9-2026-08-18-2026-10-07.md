# OONI T2: 18 Aug 2026 ММТС-9 power failure (M2) with the stage-based method

7 Oct 2026. Test T2 from `ooni-column-test-plan-2026-10-06.md`; prerequisite `coverage-check-T2-2026-10-06.md`. Code and data in `T2/`. Question: does OONI web_connectivity separate an infrastructure failure (M2: routes held, physical or transit layer down) from a filter?

## 0. Pre-registration

Written 7 Oct 2026 at 06:40 UTC, before any failure stage was extracted from the 18 Aug data. **What had already been seen**, from the coverage check of 6 Oct: the per-ASN and all-RU measurement *volume* on 17, 18 and 19 Aug by hour and by 10 minutes (the Atlas-affected group's 17:10 bin was 0.26 of 17 Aug and the following bins were above 17 Aug; all-RU 18h was 0.72 of the two-day mean, above T0's p1 of 0.594); the *upload lag* (0.06 % of 18 Aug measurements outside the pull window, none in the affected ASNs); session continuity at the onset (too few sessions to read); and one coarse peek at "all TCP endpoints failed" over all inputs in 17:10–18:10 (AS42610 0.10 against 0.02 before, n = 100; AS12714 0.38, n = 29; AS8402 0.04 flat; all-RU 0.06 against 0.05). ### 0.2 Prediction

In the affected units, failures land at **TCP connect** (or at DNS, if resolvers went down with the exchange) for domestic (.ru/.su/.рф) and foreign inputs alike, with **no domestic/foreign split**; TLS-handshake and HTTP-after-handshake failures do not rise; the test helpers may be unreachable from a probe whose uplink is gone, so `control_fail` may rise with the TCP failures. Recovery is fast: shares back under the threshold by the 18:10 bin. Control units stay flat. AS12389 Rostelecom, a national ASN, shows at most a diluted version. A domestic/foreign split, or failures *after* a completed TCP handshake (TLS `generic_timeout_error`, HTTP `timed_out`), would point to a filter instead. Quantified:

### 0.4 Hygiene, inputs, outcome, bins, thresholds

**Hygiene** as T0/T1: drop `annotations.network_type = mobile` (in 2026 only the mobile app labels it, so this removes little); keep the last measurement per (report_id, input); drop rows without `test_keys`. **VPN taint is flagged, not dropped**: the public raw data redacts `probe_ip`, so the claimed-ASN-vs-egress check cannot be made; the T0 canary proxy is used instead (a report is tainted if it measured ≥ 3 sites blocked throughout in Russia and ≥ 50 % of them reached an HTTP response) and the tainted share is reported per unit, with a sensitivity line excluding them where the share exceeds 5 %.

**Inputs** (as specified for this test): an input qualifies if, on the three baseline days after hygiene, it has ≥ 20 RU measurements and an **anomaly share < 0.05**, where anomaly is the probe-side `test_keys.blocking` flag (99.6 % agreement with the published flag, T0 D2). The 18 Aug data takes no part in the selection. Splits: domestic (.ru/.su/.рф) vs foreign; https vs http. For comparison the T1 rule (raw stage ok ≥ 90 % of control-ok) is also computed and the overlap reported; the tables use the anomaly rule.

**Outcome** per measurement = failure stage from raw `test_keys` (`T2/extract_stages.py`, byte-identical stage logic to T0/T1): `dns_fail`, `tcp_fail`, `tls_timeout`/`tls_reset`/`tls_alert`/`tls_other`, `tls_or_http_unsplit`, `http_timeout`/`http_reset`/`http_other`, `http_diff`, `ok`, with `dns_inconsistent` as a flag and `control_fail` as its own class; shares among control-ok measurements, `control_fail` among all. The `anomaly`/`blocking` fields are not the outcome.

**Bins**: 5 and 15 min on `measurement_start_time`, 60 min for the per-ASN units; baseline = the same clock bins on 17 Aug (D−1), 19 Aug (D+1) and 11 Aug (D−7). Volume per bin next to every share; all-RU volume ratio against T0's p1 (0.594, all-RU excluding the 2026-03-11 gap); for per-ASN and pooled units T0 has no usable volume threshold (tier p1 = 0), so the ratio is reported without a verdict.

**Thresholds**: T0 `thresholds_stage_share.csv` p99 by unit and n-band: all-RU rows for all-RU and for all-RU minus Moscow; per-ASN rows where T0 has them (AS12389), otherwise the ASN's tier row (tier from `coverage_quiet_days_per_asn.csv`, D if absent); pooled multi-ASN groups borrow the all-RU rows, as T1 did, and the doc says so. 5-min bins are judged against the 15-min rows. Bins with n < 20 are *no coverage*. Nothing is derived from the event data.

**Non-submission**: for every unit, the raw in-window count per hour is set against the aggregation API's count for the same ASNs and hours (`T2/raw_api/hourly_asn_18.json`, pulled 7 Oct, before this section); the difference is the share of the pipeline's own count that never reached the bucket in the window and cannot show a failure stage.

**Verdict rule per unit** (observed / null / ambiguous / no coverage, with n): *observed (M2 signature)* if P1 holds in the unit and P2 and P3 hold; *observed, filter-like* if a stage share exceeds its p99 in ≥ 1 event bin but P2 or P3 fails (failures after the handshake, or a domestic/foreign split); *ambiguous* if an exceedance occurs in one event bin and also in a baseline bin of the same clock hour, or only at 5 min; *null* if no share exceeds its p99 in 16:10–19:10 with n ≥ 20; *no coverage* if no event bin reaches n ≥ 20.

## Summary

**Verdict for the M2 × OONI cell: observed, as a destination-side signature; the vantage-side signature has no coverage.** Between 17:12 and 18:02 UTC on 18 Aug 2026, OONI probes all over Russia, including the non-Moscow control networks, failed at TCP connect (`generic_timeout_error`) on a fixed set of destinations hosted in Moscow networks. The all-RU TCP-failure share on normally-reachable inputs is 0.148 in the 17:15 bin and 0.070 for the hour against 0.002–0.007 on the three baseline days and a T0 threshold of 0.023; the same destinations were reachable before 17:10, after 18:10 and on the baseline days (4 failures in 681 measurements). In 62 of the 121 failures OONI's own test helper, outside Russia, could not connect either, so those sites were down for everyone, which no filter at Russian vantage points can produce. The hosting networks of the failing sites (JSC IOT, Datacheap, EuroByte, DINET, REG.RU) are among the ASNs whose Atlas probes dropped at the same minutes, and the DNS failures at Telecom.ru went through Hurricane Electric resolvers, another network that lost its uplink. The pre-registered verdict rule, applied mechanically, returns "observed, filter-like" for the all-RU unit, because domestic-TLD inputs failed more than foreign ones and TLS/HTTP timeouts rose a little; the input-level analysis (post hoc) shows the split follows hosting location, not jurisdiction. The networks that actually lost power, where OONI should have shown absence and helper failures, contribute six measurements in the event window: *no coverage*. Upload lag and non-submission do not enter: the pull window holds 99.8 % of the pipeline's count and 100 % in the affected ASNs.

## 10. Does OONI separate M2 from a filter here?

Partly, and not by the route the plan assumed. The plan expected the M2 signature at the *vantage* side: probes in the networks that lost power would fail at TCP on everything or fall silent. Those networks (27 ASNs, 55,637 August measurements between them, 43,654 of them MegaFon) gave six measurements in the hour, so that cell is empty, and it would be empty for any Moscow exchange failure: the networks that hang off M9 are small hosting and business ISPs where OONI has no probes. What OONI did see is the *destination* side of the same failure: the sites hosted in those networks dropped out for every Russian probe and for OONI's helper abroad, for fifty minutes, then came back. This separates M2 from the vantage-side filters in the table (M3, M4, M6): a filter at Russian ISPs cannot make the helper fail, cannot make controls in Ulyanovsk or Perm see the same failures as Moscow, and would sort destinations by jurisdiction rather than by hosting rack. It does not, on OONI's evidence alone, separate a destination-side M2 from a destination-range block (M8): both are TCP timeouts to particular IP ranges from everywhere in Russia, and for the 56 failures where the helper succeeded, OONI cannot say whether the path broke at M9 or a filter dropped the SYNs. The discriminators are the helper's own failure (62 of 121), the hosting networks coinciding with Atlas's a-root-lost ASNs, the duration (50 min against days for M8), and the pre-registered TLD split pointing the wrong way, all of which needed the input-level, post-hoc view rather than the per-unit stage shares. The honest statement for the table is that OONI identifies a Moscow infrastructure failure through what becomes unreachable, not through who loses connectivity, and that the mechanical rule, written for vantage-side signatures, misreads it as a filter.
