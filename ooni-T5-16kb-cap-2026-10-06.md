# OONI T5: the 16 KB cap (M5) — Cloudflare, June 2025

6 Oct 2026. Test T5 from `ooni-column-test-plan-2026-10-06.md`. Code in `T5/`; the raw data are the June 2025 days of the T4 pull (`T5/raw` → `T4/raw`), and the hosting classification is T4's. Pre-registered together with T4 (`ooni-T4-range-blocks-2026-10-06.md`), whose §0.2 defines the hygiene, stage, grouping and inference rules reused here.

Terms: **cap** = the 16 KB limit described below; **large input** = an input whose control body is at least 64 KiB; **small input** = control body at most 8 KiB; **stall** = TLS handshake succeeded, HTTP exchange failed; other terms as in T4.

## Summary

- **OONI shows the cap, and shows it as a size-dependent stall.** For Cloudflare-hosted inputs whose control body is at least 64 KiB, the share of control-ok measurements that complete the TLS handshake and then time out in the HTTP exchange goes from 0.1 % (6–8 June 2025) to 13.8 % (10–12 June), a difference-in-differences of +11.5 points [9.9, 13.0] against other foreign large inputs; small Cloudflare pages (≤ 8 KiB) stay at 0.6 %. Across size bins the stall share is 0.6 % below 8 KiB, 0.7 % at 8–16 KiB and 9.7 % at 16–32 KiB: the step sits at 16 KiB. In 97 % of the stalls the response headers had arrived before the timeout; timeouts outnumber resets 70 to 1. Failures before the handshake do not move (+0.4 points). Hetzner and DigitalOcean large pages behave the same way (+12.2 points).
- **The cap was not everywhere in 10–12 June.** On Rostelecom AS12389 the stall share of large Cloudflare pages reaches 42 % (DiD +36.8 points [32.3, 41.1], size contrast +40.9), which meets every part of the pre-registered *observed* rule. MGTS, MTS and SkyNet show nothing (upper bounds below 4 %), Beeline 2.8 %. The all-RU magnitude is therefore below the pre-registered 20-point line: **observed on AS12389, sub-threshold all-RU, null on MGTS/MTS/SkyNet in this window.** The step in OONI is on 10 June at 04–05 UTC, not on 9 June.
- **Bytes before the stall cannot be read from OONI.** web_connectivity 0.4.x, which every Russian measurement in June 2025 runs, logs network events only up to the TLS handshake and discards the partial body on failure. The 16 KiB figure is recovered from the control's page size instead.
- **M5 and M8 separate in OONI** (§6): the November 2024 Hetzner block (T4) sits entirely before the HTTP exchange and does not depend on page size; the June 2025 cap sits entirely after the handshake and does.

## 0. Pre-registration

Written 6 Oct 2026 at **15:35 UTC**, before any OONI data for June 2025 was pulled. What I had seen is listed in T4 §0; the part that matters here is the field inspection of T0's quiet-day files, reported in §0.1 because it is the feasibility check the brief asks for before anything else.

### 0.1 Cloudflare's publication, and what the cap is

Cloudflare, "Russian Internet users are unable to access the open Internet", blog post of 26 June 2025: "Since June 9, 2025, Internet users located in Russia and connecting to the open Internet have been throttled by Russian Internet Service Providers (ISPs)." On the mechanism: "Internal data analysis suggests that the throttling allows Internet users to load only the first 16 KB of any web asset, rendering most web navigation impossible." It covers "all connection methods and protocols, including HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2 on TCP and TLS, as well as HTTP/3 on QUIC", reports "an increase of TCP connections being 'reset' prematurely", and names Rostelecom, Megafon, Vimpelcom, MTS and MGTS. **Onset: 9 June 2025.** The ntc.party thread 17013 (18 Jun 2025) describes the same stall after about 16 KB for Cloudflare, Hetzner and DigitalOcean, one reply putting it "at 20 KB". The mechanism table's M5 row cites forum scans (17013 #886, 4625 #1526) that were not read for this pre-registration.

### 0.2 Step 1, feasibility: what web_connectivity records about bytes received

Checked on T0's already-downloaded quiet-day files (two objects from upload hour 12 on each of 2024-09-18, 2025-02-19, 2025-09-21 and 2026-06-10; 12,365 measurements), before pulling anything new. Every measurement runs `test_version` 0.4.x (0.4.3 in 92 % of the 2025 sample, 0.4.0–0.4.2 the rest; no 0.5.x). Findings:

1. `control.http_request.body_length` (the test helper's full body length) is present in 100 % of measurements; `control.dns.addrs` likewise. So **large and small pages can be told apart by the control.**
2. `requests[].response.body` is a snapshot capped at 128 KiB (observed maximum 131,891 characters) with `body_is_truncated`. **When the HTTP exchange fails, the snapshot is empty** (body length 0 in every failure sample except one page that timed out after filling the 128 KiB snapshot). `body_proportion` is then 0. So the probe does not keep the partial body.
3. `network_events` carry `num_bytes` for `read` and `write` operations, but in 0.4.x they cover **only the TCP connect and TLS handshake phase**: in every sample the last event is the 24–31-byte write right after `tls_handshake_done`, and no request write or body read follows, whether the HTTP exchange succeeded or failed. So **bytes received before a stall cannot be counted** from the public data.
4. What is recorded about a stall: `http_experiment_failure` (`generic_timeout_error`, `timed_out`, `connection_reset`, `unknown_failure: stream error …`), and the first response's status `code`, which is > 0 when the headers arrived before the body stalled and 0 when nothing arrived. In the quiet-day sample, 7 of 216 HTTP failures after a good handshake have a code; under a 16 KB cap, large pages should fail with a code (headers are within the first 16 KB).

**Decision.** The strong form of feasibility (count bytes before the stall) fails; the weak form the brief allows (tell large from small responses) holds. The prediction "bytes received clustered near 16 KB" is therefore **untestable in these data** and is replaced, before pulling, by a size-gradient design: the stall share as a function of the control body length, which a 16 KB cap must make step-shaped and which a connect-stage block (M8) or a general outage must leave flat. The share of RU measurements on the chosen days carrying the needed fields (`test_version` 0.4.x, `control.http_request.body_length` ≥ 0, `tls_handshakes` recorded) is reported in §2.

### 0.3 Step 2, design

**Days.** As T4 E2: baseline 2–4 Jun 2025 (selection and size classification), before 6–8 Jun, onset 9 Jun (hourly series only), after 10–12 Jun.

**Inputs.** T4's strict set and balanced panel, https inputs, hosting from the control's addresses (T4 §0.2). The input's **size** is the median `control.http_request.body_length` over its control-ok baseline measurements. Cut-offs, fixed now: **large ≥ 65,536 B** (primary), **small ≤ 8,192 B** (passes a 16 KB cap even with headers), and the bins < 8 KiB, 8–16, 16–32, 32–64, ≥ 64 KiB for the gradient. Sensitivity: large ≥ 32,768 B. Inputs with control body length −1 or 0 (control failed or empty) are excluded from the size analysis.

**Groups.** Cloudflare large vs other-foreign large (primary); Cloudflare small vs other-foreign small; Hetzner + DigitalOcean large (the forum says they share the cap); domestic large as the second control. Before vs after, all-RU and the seven usable ASNs, with the T4 estimator (cluster bootstrap over inputs, Clopper–Pearson for n < 200).

**Outcome.** Stall family = `http_timeout`, `http_reset`, `http_other` among control-ok measurements with a recorded successful handshake, split by `http_code > 0`. Blocked family as in T4. The stall share by size bin is the **gradient**.

**Primary quantities.** (a) DiD(stall) = (Cloudflare large after − before) − (other-foreign large after − before). (b) The size contrast after onset within Cloudflare: stall share of large minus stall share of small inputs. (c) DiD(blocked) for Cloudflare large, which must stay near 0 for the M5 reading.

**Verdict rule for the M5 × OONI cell.** *Observed* if DiD(stall) ≥ 0.20 with the interval excluding 0, the size contrast (b) ≥ 0.20 with the interval excluding 0, and DiD(blocked) < 0.05. *Ambiguous* if DiD(stall) ≥ 0.20 but the size contrast is below 0.20 (then the data show a failure, not a cap), or if the blocked family rises by ≥ 0.05 as well. *Null* if DiD(stall) < 0.05 or its interval includes 0. *No coverage* if Cloudflare large has fewer than 10 inputs or fewer than 200 control-ok measurements in either period.

## 6. Does OONI separate M5 from M8?

Yes, in these two events, on two independent axes.

| | Nov 2024 Hetzner (T4, M8 anchor) | June 2025 cap (T5, M5 anchor) |
|---|---|---|
| Where the failure sits | Before the HTTP exchange: blocked family +0.044 all-RU, +0.118 AS50716; stall family −0.001 | After the handshake: stall family +0.078 to +0.139 all-RU, +0.24 to +0.34 Rostelecom; blocked family ≤ +0.006 |
| Composition of the rise | TCP ok → TLS timeout 71 %, TCP fail 28 %; plain-http inputs fail at TCP | `http_timeout` 97 %, headers already received 97 % (Cloudflare), 83 % (Hetzner); resets 1 % |
| Page size | Not tested in T4 (no reason to); the http inputs fail at TCP regardless of size | Step at 16 KiB: ≤ 16 KiB pages 0.6–0.7 %, > 16 KiB pages 10–17 % |
| Test-helper exchange (small, to DigitalOcean) | unaffected (DO not targeted) | unaffected (`control_fail` ≤ 0.3 %) although DO pages stall: the cap passes small transfers |

The table's discriminator, "M8 fails at connect/handshake, M5 passes the handshake and stalls the transfer", is what the data show, with one correction: in OONI the M8 block appears mostly as a handshake timeout after a successful TCP connect, not as a connect failure, so the line to draw is before versus after the handshake, not connect versus handshake. The second discriminator, page size, is only available for M5 and only through the control's body length, since OONI does not count bytes; it is what makes the cap identifiable as a cap rather than as a generic post-handshake failure (the other-foreign group's small post-handshake rise in June, +0.022, has no size step and no provider).

What OONI cannot do: distinguish a 16 KiB cap from a cap at some other size in this range (the bins are coarse and the control length is the decompressed body); say whether the stall is a drop or a shaping delay (the probe's read timeout fires first); and see either mechanism on a network whose probes did not run into it, which in both events was most of the large national ASNs.

## 8. Open questions

1. **Staged roll-out or probe placement?** Rostelecom, Ufanet and ER-Telecom Kazan show the cap at 28–62 % within two days; MGTS, MegaFon AS12714 and MTS AS8359 show nothing. Cloudflare named MGTS and MTS. Either their TSPU applied the cap later than 12 June, or their OONI probes sit on segments without it. A pull of 16–20 June would answer the first; the forum thread 17013 has per-ISP reports to compare with.
2. **The 16 KiB figure.** The step between the 8–16 and 16–32 KiB bins is consistent with 16 KiB but also with anything from ~12 to ~20 KiB (one forum reply says "at 20 KB"); the bins and the compression gap do not allow better. Finer bins on the 16–32 KiB inputs (103 inputs) could narrow it, with the caveat that the probe sees compressed bytes and the control reports decompressed length.
3. **Why do 3 % of Cloudflare stalls have no headers?** Either the cap is counted on the TCP stream including the TLS handshake (large certificate chains could consume the budget before the headers on some sites), or these are unrelated failures at the baseline rate (0.1–0.2 %); the latter is about the right size.
4. **The standing-condition question.** The mechanism table calls M5 a standing condition. ntc.party 17013 #886 reports mass deployments on 27 Dec 2025 and 14 Jan 2026, and 4625 #1526 (19 Apr 2026) finds 67 of 105 hosting endpoints stalling at 16 KB from central Russia. T0 found the 2026 quiet-day baseline raised through HTTP timeouts. The size-gradient method here can be run on T0's 2026 quiet days to test whether that rise is the cap; it needs no new pull.
