6 Oct 2026. Test T1 from `ooni-column-test-plan-2026-10-06.md`. Code and data are in `T1/`. T0 (`ooni-T0-calibration-2026-10-06.md`) supplies the thresholds; its API half is still provisional, which does not affect T1's verdict (see §0.3).

Written 6 Oct 2026 at 15:16 UTC, before any 14 January data was pulled for this test. What I had seen: the plan, the T0 result doc and its derived tables, the four context docs (in particular `jan14-2025-dissection-2026-10-05.md`, which analysed the same event with the OONI API's anomaly flag and a hand sample of raw measurements), and one S3 listing giving the size of the 14 Jan RU prefix (72 jsonl.gz objects, 1.18 GB).

### 0.1 Design, fixed in advance

**Event window.** Onset 14:03 UTC, recovery 14:29–14:31 UTC (dissection §1, from Atlas and press). Event window 14:00–14:35 UTC; with the plan's ±60 min margin the analysis window is **13:00–15:35 UTC**, reported as 60-min bins 13:00–15:59, 15-min bins, and 5-min bins 13:55–14:35 for a direct comparison with the dissection's table.

**Data.** Raw web_connectivity jsonl for RU from the public bucket, for 14 Jan and its baseline days 13 Jan (D−1), 15 Jan (D+1) and 7 Jan (D−7), each as upload-day D plus upload hours 00–05 of D+1, exactly as T0. No API call is needed for the verdict.

**Unit.** All-RU; each ASN with ≥ 20 measurements in a bin; two named groups: *reported affected* = AS12389, 25513, 8331, 49346, 50716, 35728 (raw samples in the dissection) and AS8359, 3216, 8402, 31200 (Atlas probes failed), and *within-country control* = the 17 ASNs whose Atlas probes succeeded during the event (38984, 42289, 59734, 8985, 51724, 49828, 48149, 20764, 59504, 35807, 39503, 31275, 35237, 210756, 57073, 209847, 48573). The control group tests whether OONI sees the networks Atlas saw as unaffected.

**Hygiene.** As T0: drop `network_type = mobile`; keep the last measurement per (report_id, input); drop measurements without `test_keys`; flag and exclude canary-tainted reports (≥ 3 canary inputs, ≥ 50 % reaching stage ok), reporting their share.

**Inputs.** Plan step 4 as amended on 6 Oct: an input qualifies if, over the three baseline days after hygiene, it has ≥ 20 RU measurements and raw stage `ok` in ≥ 90 % of its control-ok measurements. This is the first test that can use the plan's definition as written, because the baseline days are pulled raw. The 14 Jan data takes no part in the selection. Splits: domestic (.ru/.su/.рф) vs foreign, https vs http.

**Outcome.** The T0 stage (`extract_stages.py`, unchanged logic), with `control_fail` as its own class and the other shares computed among control-ok measurements, as in T0 §8.

**Thresholds.** From `T0/derived/thresholds_stage_share.csv` (60-min and 15-min, all-RU and per tier/band) and `thresholds_volume_ratio.csv` (all-RU p1 = 0.575). A bin "exceeds" when its share is above the p99 for its unit and n-band. Bins with n < 20 are "no coverage". Volume ratio = bin count on 14 Jan ÷ mean of the same bin on the three baseline days (hygiene set, all inputs). The non-Russian volume control (T0 consequence 4) is `s3_volume.sh`: bytes and objects per upload hour for RU, US, DE and BR on the four days, compared against the same listing for T0's ten quiet days.

**Verdict rule for the M6 × OONI cell.** *Observed* if, in the all-RU unit, (a) the TLS-stage failure share among control-ok https measurements exceeds the T0 p99 in at least two consecutive 15-min bins inside 14:00–14:35 and in no 15-min bin of the same clock window on the three baseline days, and (b) the volume ratio of at least one 15-min bin inside the window is below 0.575 while the US/DE/BR upload volume for upload hours 14–15 UTC stays within the quiet-day range. *Ambiguous* if (a) holds but (b) does not, or the reverse. *Null* if neither. Per-ASN cells use the same rule with tier thresholds, or *no coverage*.

## Summary

**Verdict for the M6 × OONI cell: observed.** With the stage-based method, 14 Jan 2025 reproduces both pre-registered signals: the TLS-stage timeout share on normally-reachable foreign https inputs is 0.75 in the core window against 0.0015 on the baseline days, and the all-RU measurement volume falls to 0.16 of baseline in the 14:15 bin while US, German and Brazilian upload volume holds. Plain-http inputs time out at the HTTP stage, TCP connects succeed throughout, and the test helpers fail from the Russian side. The event is visible at minute resolution: timeouts start between 14:01:34 and 14:03:01 UTC, about a minute before Atlas's first observed failure, and end between 14:28 and 14:33 UTC. Eight of ten predictions held; the two misses are about magnitude (the TLS share in 15-minute bins is diluted by http inputs and pre-onset minutes) and a residual on МГТС half an hour after recovery.

Two findings about the instrument: the volume drop is largely a throughput effect, since each measurement in a running session waits out its timeout (median 1.9 → 0.4 measurements per minute per session), and the probe-side anomaly flag misses a third of the failures (HTTP timeouts on plain http and every test-helper failure), which hid the failure of AS3216 in the 5 Oct dissection. Per-ASN coverage is the limit: in a 27-minute event only Rostelecom reaches 20 measurements.

## 9. Consequences for T2–T7 and for the table

1. **The method validates.** Stage-specific shares among control-ok measurements, `control_fail` as its own class, inputs selected from raw baseline days, and the T0 thresholds together reproduce the 14 Jan signature with margin, and the verdict rule of §0.1 returns *observed* without judgement calls. The plan can proceed to T2.
2. **Coverage, not sensitivity, is the constraint for per-ASN cells.** A 27-minute nationwide event gives one ASN with n ≥ 20. T2 (one exchange, hours) and T3/T7 (regional ISPs) should check `coverage_asn_by_month.csv` and expect *no coverage* in most per-ASN cells; the all-RU unit and pooled groups are what OONI can answer.
3. **A within-country control group needs to be chosen from OONI's own coverage**, not from Atlas's probe list: none of the 17 Atlas-unaffected ASNs measured during the event.
4. **The volume drop is not an independent signal.** It is mostly the throughput loss of sessions waiting out timeouts, so it will accompany any mechanism that produces timeouts (M6, and M3/M4 if they time out rather than reset) and will be weaker for mechanisms that fail fast (resets, refused connects). For T2, predict a volume drop only if the failures are timeouts.
5. **Timing at minute resolution is available** from the per-minute timeout series on F, finer than Atlas's hourly built-ins. For T2 and T4 the onset minute can be compared with external reports.
6. **The OONI column of the mechanism table**: M6 is now **obs.** The cell text proposed for `mechanism-instrument-table-fixed-line-2026-09-29.md` (the canonical file is not on this machine; the copy in `context/` has been edited): *obs. (T1, 14 Jan 2025): TLS timeouts 0.75 on foreign https, 0.64 domestic; HTTP timeouts on plain http; TCP ok; helper failures 0.11; all-RU volume 0.16 of baseline, US/DE/BR steady; onset 14:01:34–14:03:01, recovery 14:28–14:33 UTC; n = 94 in the core window; anomaly flag misses a third of the failures.*
